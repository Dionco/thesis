Figure~\ref{fig:comparison_cristofari} then compares our parameters specifically with those of \citet{cristofari_2023b}, which isolates the difference between optical and NIR retrievals most directly, since both analyses rely on a similar model grid, fitting procedure and choice of fundamental parameters.

Overall, our atmospheric parameters agree with the literature. For $T_{\rm eff}$, $\log g$, and $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ the mean offset is small and consistently above the published values: $20$~K, $0.13$~dex, and $0.14$~dex, respectively. In each case this offset does not exceed the mean star-by-star scatter among the published sources, $42$~K, $0.13$~dex, and $0.18$~dex, respectively, so our systematic is no larger than the disagreement between existing catalogues.

As in \citet{cristofari_2023b}, we find $\log g$ to be among the hardest parameters to constrain. As a consistency check, we compared our spectroscopic $\log g$ with values calculated from $M_\star$ and $R_\star$. For each star, we computed the radius from the retrieved $T_{\rm eff}$ and luminosity via the Stefan--Boltzmann law, and the mass from the mass--magnitude--metallicity relation of \citet{Mann_2015}. Combining the two yields $\log g_\mathrm{SB}$. Figure~\ref{fig:comparison_logg_cristofari} compares these estimates with our retrieved values. The two are mostly consistent. As expected from \citet{Maldonado_2015}, $\log g$ scales inversely with $T_{\rm eff}$ across the sample. Our values track the $\log g_\mathrm{SB}$ estimates more closely than those of \citet{cristofari_2023b}, which may reflect improvements in the atmospheric models in recent years and, in turn, more reliable $\log g$ constraints.

\citet{cristofari_2023b, reiners+2022} report average magnetic fields for most of our targets, included in the comparisons of Figs.~\ref{fig:literature_params} and~\ref{fig:comparison_cristofari}. The contrast with \citet{cristofari_2023b} is again instructive: our $\langle B \rangle$ values are measured from ESPaDOnS and NARVAL spectra in the optical, whereas theirs derive from SPIRou data in the NIR. We find that the mean offset is above 0.60 kG above the published values, several times the 0.13 kG scatter between those two sources, with differences up to 2.6 kG (GJ 1103). These differences can stem from several modelling steps, such as the models used, the line list the analyses rely on, the fitting procedure, magnetic component spacing, and the choice of fundamental parameters for analysed targets. 

The studies do agree on which stars are most magnetic: we, \citet{reiners+2022}, and \citet{cristofari_2023b} all rank GJ~1286, GJ~1002, GJ~1151, GJ~1289, GJ~873, Gl~388, and Gl~410 among the most magnetic stars in the sample. This is also consistent with \citet{Schofer_2019}, who measured H$\alpha$ equivalent widths for many of our targets. The clearest disagreements are Gl~905 and GJ~1103, which we find to be more active than these works suggest. Our estimated magnetic fields go as low as 0.30 kG (Gl 514) and does not reach values as low as \citet{cristofari_2023b}.

Since the field is driven by a rotation-dependent dynamo, we place our targets in the $\langle B\rangle$--Rossby-number plane. We adopt the Rossby numbers of \citet{cristofari_2023b}, based on rotation periods from \citet{fouque_2023} and \citet{Donati_2023}, and Fig.~\ref{fig:rossby} shows the 35 stars with an available $\mathrm{Ro}$ alongside the samples of \citet{cristofari_2023b} and \citet{reiners+2022}. All our targets fall in the unsaturated dynamo regime ($\mathrm{Ro}>0.13$), with the exception of the rapidly rotating active M dwarf GJ 873 ($\mathrm{Ro}\approx0.07$, $\langle B\rangle\approx4\,\mathrm{kG}$), which lies in the saturated regime and anchors the low-$\mathrm{Ro}$ end of the diagram. This is consistent with their long rotation periods, earlier activity estimates \citep{fouque_2018,Schofer_2019}, and the moderate fields we retrieve ($\langle B\rangle = 0.3$--$4.0\,\mathrm{kG}$, median $0.8\,\mathrm{kG}$). Most have $\mathrm{Ro}<2$; the exceptions are Gl~408 ($\mathrm{Ro}=2.7$) and Gl~411, whose period is the longest in the sample ($P_{\rm rot}=427\pm41\,\mathrm{d}$) and which sits apart at $\mathrm{Ro}=6.9$.

In the unsaturated regime the field is expected to fall steeply with Rossby number, as $\langle B\rangle\propto\mathrm{Ro}^{-1.26}$ \citep{reiners+2022}. Our retrieved fields show no such trend: they scatter considerably and yield a slope consistent with zero ($\propto\mathrm{Ro}^{0.0\pm0.2}$), so across our sample $\langle B\rangle$ has no significant dependence on $\mathrm{Ro}$.

\subsection{BIC selection}
\label{sec:results_bic}
For all 41 stars we compare the averaged field $\langle B\rangle$ from the run with all magnetic components against the BIC-selected (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_bic}) run (Fig.~\ref{fig:bic_field_comparison}). The selection lowers $\langle B\rangle$ for 35 stars and raises it for the remaining 6, with a median change of $-0.12\,\mathrm{kG}$. This shift is well within the typical $1\sigma$ uncertainty on $\langle B\rangle$ ($\approx0.28\,\mathrm{kG}$), and smaller than it for 38 of the 41 stars. No shift is outside of $2\sigma$, and the field is driven to zero for no star. The largest single excursions are $+0.75\,\mathrm{kG}$ for GJ~1103 and reductions of $-0.50\,\mathrm{kG}$ for Gl~169.1\,A and $-0.48\,\mathrm{kG}$ for Gl~445, all within their error bars. For stars with stronger detected fields (e.g.\ Gl~873, Gl~388) the full and BIC-selected fields agree to well within their uncertainties and these findings also agree with \citet{reiners+2022}. The selection therefore trims the weakly constrained strong-field components without disturbing the strong-field measurements.

Because the magnetic and atmospheric parameters are sampled jointly, a more accurate $\langle B \rangle$ can in turn refine the other atmospheric parameters. Tightening the magnetic model is therefore expected to shift $T_{\rm eff}$, $\log g$, and $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ slightly, improving rather than biasing them. We test this by comparing the same two runs parameter by parameter (Figs.~\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_teff}--\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_mh}), both using the same region-filtered line lists. Each figure shows the per-star values in both runs and, in the lower panel, their difference.. The median shifts are $-14\,\mathrm{K}$ in $T_{\rm eff}$, $+0.04$~dex in $\log g$, and $+0.03$~dex in $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, each below the typical per-star uncertainty ($\approx20\,\mathrm{K}$, $0.06$~dex, and $0.04$~dex) and showing no systematic offset across the sample. A few stars stand out in the $\Delta$ panels, with $T_{\rm eff}$ offsets as large as $\sim60\,\mathrm{K}$ (Gl~687, Gl~846, Gl~725~A), but these remain within $\sim2\sigma$ and are driven partly by the larger uncertainty of the BIC-selected run rather than a genuine parameter shift. We conclude that the BIC selection removes the unsupported strong-field components and feeds back onto the atmospheric parameters only at the level of a slight refinement, consistent with the joint sampling and well below the per-star uncertainties.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We apply our process to the 41 targets in our sample and report the retrieved atmospheric parameters and average magnetic field strengths in Table~\ref{tab:results}.
An example of a best-fit model compared to the observation data is represented in Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral_fit_ds_leo}, which shows a selection of regions which have been used to compute the likelihood for star Gl~410.

\citet{cristofari_2023b, reiners+2022} report average magnetic fields for most of our targets, included in the comparisons of Figs.~\ref{fig:literature_params} and~\ref{fig:comparison_cristofari}. The contrast with \citet{cristofari_2023b} is again instructive: our $\langle B\rangle$ values are measured from ESPaDOnS and NARVAL spectra in the optical, whereas theirs derive from SPIRou data in the NIR. We find that the mean offset is 0.60 kG above the published values, several times the 0.13 kG scatter between those two sources, with differences up to 2.6 kG (GJ 1103). These differences can stem from several modelling steps, such as the models used, the line list the analyses rely on, the fitting procedure, magnetic component spacing, and the choice of fundamental parameters for analysed targets. 

The studies do agree on which stars are most magnetic: we, \citet{reiners+2022}, and \citet{cristofari_2023b} all rank GJ~1286, GJ~1002, GJ~1151, GJ~1289, GJ~873, Gl~388, and Gl~410 among the most magnetic stars in the sample. This is also consistent with \citet{Schofer_2019}, who measured H$\alpha$ equivalent widths for many of our targets. Our estimated magnetic fields go as low as 0.30 kG (Gl 514) but do not reach values as low as those of \citet{cristofari_2023b}.

Since the field is driven by a rotation-dependent dynamo, we place our targets in the $\langle B\rangle$--Rossby-number plane. We adopt the Rossby numbers of \citet{cristofari_2023b}, based on rotation periods from \citet{fouque_2023} and \citet{Donati_2023}, and Fig.~\ref{fig:rossby} shows the 35 stars with an available $\mathrm{Ro}$ alongside the samples of \citet{cristofari_2023b} and \citet{reiners+2022}. All our targets fall in the unsaturated dynamo regime ($\mathrm{Ro}>0.13$), with the exception of the rapidly rotating active M dwarf GJ 873 ($\mathrm{Ro}\approx0.07$, $\langle B\rangle\approx4\,\mathrm{kG}$), which lies in the saturated regime and anchors the low-$\mathrm{Ro}$ end of the diagram. This is consistent with their long rotation periods, earlier activity estimates \citep{fouque_2018,Schofer_2019}, and the moderate fields we retrieve ($\langle B\rangle = 0.3$--$4.0\,\mathrm{kG}$, median $0.8\,\mathrm{kG}$). Most have $\mathrm{Ro}<2$; the exceptions are Gl~408 ($\mathrm{Ro}=2.7$) and Gl~411, whose period is the longest in the sample ($P_\mathrm{rot}=427\pm41\,\mathrm{d}$) and which sits apart at $\mathrm{Ro}=6.9$.

In the unsaturated regime the field is expected to fall steeply with Rossby number, as $\langle B\rangle\propto\mathrm{Ro}^{-1.26}$ \citep{reiners+2022}. Our retrieved fields show no such trend: they scatter considerably and yield a slope consistent with zero ($\propto\mathrm{Ro}^{0.0\pm0.2}$), so across our sample $\langle B\rangle$ has no significant dependence on $\mathrm{Ro}$.

\label{sec:results_bic}
\begin{figure}

      \caption{Magnetic components removed by the BIC scan per star, out of the full ten-component grid. Components are pruned for 40 of 41 stars (median 4); only GJ\,1103 retains all ten.}

\begin{figure*}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/magnetic_strength_pre_and_post_bic_scan_residuals.pdf}
    \caption{Average magnetic field $\langle B\rangle$ for each of the 41 stars from the full-magnetic run (open grey) and the BIC-selected run (red), ordered by decreasing full-magnetic $\langle B\rangle$; the vertical segment marks the field added or removed by the component selection. The selection lowers $\langle B\rangle$ for most stars and leaves every star with a non-zero field, each change staying within the $1\sigma$ error bars.}
    \label{fig:bic_field_comparison}
\end{figure*}

For all 41 stars we compare the averaged field $\langle B\rangle$ from the run with all magnetic components against the BIC-selected (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_bic}) run (Fig.~\ref{fig:bic_field_comparison}). The selection lowers $\langle B\rangle$ for 35 stars and raises it for the remaining 6, with a median change of $-0.12\,\mathrm{kG}$. This shift is well within the typical $1\sigma$ uncertainty on $\langle B\rangle$ ($\approx0.28\,\mathrm{kG}$), and smaller than that uncertainty for 38 of the 41 stars. No shift is outside of $2\sigma$, and the field is driven to zero for no star. The distribution of magnetic components trimmed per star is presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:bic_components_removed_hist}. For 40\% of the stars, 4 magnetic components (max $\langle B\rangle$ = 6 kG) are removed. The largest single excursions are $+0.75\,\mathrm{kG}$ for GJ~1103 and reductions of $-0.50\,\mathrm{kG}$ for Gl~169.1\,A and $-0.48\,\mathrm{kG}$ for Gl~445, all within their error bars. For stars with stronger detected fields (e.g.\ Gl~873, Gl~388) the full and BIC-selected fields agree to well within their uncertainties and these findings also agree with \citet{reiners+2022}. The selection therefore trims the weakly constrained strong-field components without disturbing the strong-field measurements.

Because the magnetic and atmospheric parameters are sampled jointly, a more accurate $\langle B\rangle$ can in turn refine the other atmospheric parameters. Tightening the magnetic model is therefore expected to shift $T_\mathrm{eff}$, $\log g$, and $[\mathrm{M/H}]$ slightly, improving rather than biasing them. We test this by comparing the same two runs parameter by parameter (App.~\ref{app:atmo_pre_post_bic}, Figs.~\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_teff}--\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_mh}), both using the same region-filtered line lists. Each figure shows the per-star values in both runs and, in the lower panel, their difference. The median shifts are $-14\,\mathrm{K}$ in $T_\mathrm{eff}$, $+0.04$~dex in $\log g$, and $+0.03$~dex in $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, each below the typical per-star uncertainty ($\approx20\,\mathrm{K}$, $0.06$~dex, and $0.04$~dex) and showing no systematic offset across the sample. A few stars stand out in the $\Delta$ panels, with $T_\mathrm{eff}$ offsets as large as $\sim60\,\mathrm{K}$ (Gl~687, Gl~846, Gl~725~A), but these remain within $\sim2\sigma$ and are driven partly by the larger uncertainty of the BIC-selected run rather than a genuine parameter shift. We conclude that the BIC selection removes the unsupported strong-field components and feeds back onto the atmospheric parameters only at the level of a slight refinement, consistent with the joint sampling and well below the per-star uncertainties.

\subsection{Region filtering}
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/region_filtering_stats_histogram.pdf}
    \caption{Number of line regions discarded by the filtering step (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_filter}), across the 41-star sample. A region (one of the 29 windows in the used line list) is dropped when its reduced $\chi^2$ exceeds 50. The cut is mild for most stars: 16 lose none, the median star loses one ($\sim$3\%) and the mean is a cut of 5 regions ($\sim$17\%). A tail of magnetically active stars is filtered far more heavily, with four (Gl~15~B, Gl~725~B, Gl~15~A, Gl~687) losing more than 60\% of regions.}
    \label{fig:region_filtering_stats_histogram}
\end{figure}

The line-filtering step (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_filter}) removes regions that the forward model reproduces poorly, for instance because of tellurics. Figure~\ref{fig:region_filtering_stats_histogram} shows how many regions are removed per star. For most stars, little is taken out: 16 ($\sim$40\%) keep all 29 regions, and $\sim$68\% keep more than 90\% of theirs. The median star loses just one region (3\%). A few stars, however, are filtered much more heavily, pulling the mean up to $\sim$5 regions (17\%). Four stars make up this tail (Gl~15~B, Gl~725~B, Gl~15~A and Gl~687) each losing more than 60\% of their regions. All four were assigned $T_\mathrm{eff}\approx3000$\,K in the diagnostic run, at the cool edge of our model grid. In the full run after filtering, their temperatures move higher (Table~\ref{tab:results}).

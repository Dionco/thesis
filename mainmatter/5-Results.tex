\subsection{BIC selection}
\label{sec:results_bic}
The BIC scan (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_bic}) fixes the number of magnetic filling-factor components per star, dropping the highest-field components that the spectrum does not constrain. The selection lowers $\langle B\rangle$ for all 40 stars (Fig.~\ref{fig:bic_field_comparison}), confirming the one-sided upward bias of the full grid.
% RECONCILE: figures and Ch.4 text use 40 stars incl. Gl 873; this table has 39. Dion to resolve.
The shift is systematically downwards with a median of $0.23\,\mathrm{kG}$, comparable to the typical $1\sigma$ uncertainty on $\langle B\rangle$ and smaller than the error bar for 24 of the 40 stars.
% RECONCILE: figures and Ch.4 text use 40 stars incl. Gl 873; this table has 39. Dion to resolve.
It is largest in the weakly magnetic tail, where the non-negativity bias dominates. Every shift above $2\sigma$ falls there, and the field is removed entirely for six stars with full-grid $\langle B\rangle \lesssim 0.7\,\mathrm{kG}$. For the genuinely magnetic stars the full and BIC-selected models agree within their uncertainties, so the BIC component selection suppresses the weak-field signal without disturbing the strong-field measurements.

\begin{figure*}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/bic_scan_field_comparison.pdf}
    \caption{Average magnetic field $\langle B \rangle$ for each star from the full-magnetic run (open grey) and the BIC-selected run (red), ordered by decreasing $\langle B \rangle$. The selection lowers $\langle B \rangle$ for all stars, and removes the field entirely for six weakly magnetic ones (blue triangles), each vertical bar showing the spurious field it strips. This is the sample-wide counterpart of the single-star scan shown for Gl~410 in Fig.~\ref{fig:bic_scan_ds_leo}.}
    \label{fig:bic_field_comparison}
\end{figure*}

Because the magnetic and atmospheric parameters are sampled jointly, reducing the magnetic model could in principle propagate into the atmospheric solution. If dropping components shifted $T_{\rm eff}$, $\log g$ or $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, the component selection would be biasing the atmospheric parameters rather than only lowering $\langle B \rangle$.

We test this by retrieving every star twice: a full-magnetic run, with all magnetic filling-factor components, and a BIC-selected run, with the number of components fixed to the value chosen by the scan. Figures~\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_teff}--\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_mh} compare the two runs parameter by parameter, showing each star's value in both runs and, in the lower panel, their difference. The median shifts are $+2.3$~K in $T_{\rm eff}$, $-0.02$~dex in $\log g$ and $-0.002$~dex in $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, each below the per-star uncertainties and with no trend across the sample. A few stars stand out in the $\Delta$ panels: for Gl~411, Gl~48, and Gl~873 the filtered line lists differed between the full-magnetic and BIC-selected runs, because the diagnostic fit that sets the filter depends on the magnetic configuration (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_filter}). Their larger shifts therefore reflect a change in the fitted regions rather than the component selection itself. Pending a rerun on a common line list, we conclude that at fixed regions the BIC selection removes the unsupported high-field components without measurably altering the atmospheric parameters.

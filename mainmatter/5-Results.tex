\subsection{BIC selection}
\label{sec:results_bic}
The BIC scan (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_bic}) decides how many magnetic filling-factor components the data actually support and drops the highest-field components that the spectrum does not constrain. This raises an obvious question: does simplifying the magnetic model in this way feed back into the stellar atmosphere? If removing components shifted $T_{\rm eff}$, $\log g$ or $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, the magnetic-component choice would be contaminating the atmospheric solution rather than just cleaning up the field.

To check this we retrieve every star twice: a \emph{full-magnetic} run, with all magnetic filling-factor components left free, and a \emph{BIC-selected} run, with the number of components fixed to the value picked by the scan. Figures~\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_teff}--\ref{fig:pre_post_bic_mh} compare the two runs parameter by parameter, plotting each star's value in both runs and, in the lower panel, their difference. The shifts are negligible: the median change is $+2.3$~K in $T_{\rm eff}$, $-0.02$~dex in $\log g$ and $-0.002$~dex in $[\mathrm{M/H}]$, in every case far smaller than the per-star uncertainties and with no trend across the sample. The BIC selection therefore removes the unsupported high-field components without measurably altering the atmospheric parameters, so the magnetic and atmospheric parts of the fit are effectively decoupled.

\section{Observations and data reduction}

This chapter describes the spectroscopic data underlying the thesis. We work in two wavelength regimes: the optical, with NARVAL and ESPaDOnS spectra drawn from the PolarBase archive, and the near-infrared, with SPIRou spectra. Both samples are extended by the new-targets campaign, which adds less-studied M~dwarfs not analysed in \citet{cristofari+2022b, cristofari_2023b}. Throughout this work we analyse only the Stokes I (unpolarised intensity) component of the spectropolarimetric observations. The magnetic measurements presented here rely on Zeeman intensification, which probes the total unsigned surface magnetic flux through differential broadening of magnetically sensitive lines in Stokes I. This diagnostic is complementary to circular polarimetry. Stokes V is sensitive only to the net line-of-sight field and is therefore restricted to the large-scale, signed component, whereas mixed-polarity small-scale fields, which dominate the magnetic flux budget of M dwarfs, cancel in polarised light but contribute fully to the Stokes I signal \citep{reiners_basri_2009, Kochukhov_2021}.

\subsection{Instruments and reduction}
\label{sec:obs_instruments}

We use PolarBase\footnote{\url{https://www.polarbase.ovgso.fr/}} \citep{polarbase1, polarbase2}, a publicly available archive of high-resolution spectropolarimetric stellar observations with extensive coverage of M~dwarfs.

In the optical, we use NARVAL and ESPaDOnS spectra, both of which cover 0.37--1\,$\mu$m at a resolving power of ${\sim}65{,}000$ \citep{narval, espadons, polarbase1}. NARVAL and ESPaDOnS data are reduced in real time at the Télescope Bernard Lyot (TBL) and the Canada--France--Hawaii Telescope (CFHT), respectively, using the Libre-Esprit pipeline, which implements the algorithm of \citet{polarbase2}. An initial wavelength solution is derived from a Th--Ar spectrum acquired during the night and subsequently refined using telluric bands superimposed on each stellar spectrum as radial-velocity references, yielding a final radial-velocity accuracy of $20$--$30$\,m\,s$^{-1}$ \citep{instrument_reduction_pipeline}. The pipeline also automatically normalises the continuum.

\subsection{Per-star data preparation}
\label{sec:obs_preparation}

For each star, we correct the observed spectra for the barycentric Earth radial velocity (BERV) and discard any observation with ${\rm SNR}<100$ to avoid contaminating our results with low-quality data. The remaining spectra acquired within a single night are cubically interpolated onto a common wavelength grid and median-combined pixel-by-pixel to produce a nightly template. We then median-combine all nightly templates to construct a final template for each star. The combination across nights and epochs implicitly assumes that the magnetic configuration of each star is stationary on the relevant timescales; we refer to [CITATION NEEDED: Reiners+2022, arXiv:2204.00342] for empirical bounds on the epoch-to-epoch variability of M-dwarf surface fields, which are below the systematic-uncertainty floor of our retrieval.

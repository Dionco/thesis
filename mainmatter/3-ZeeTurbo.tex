\texttt{ZeeTurbo} is a spectral-synthesis code that computes the emergent spectrum of a star for a given set of atmospheric parameters and a given surface magnetic field strength \citep{cristofari_2023a}. We use it to generate the synthetic spectra that the pipeline (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods}) compares against the observations, and in particular to model the Zeeman broadening from which we measure the field (Sect.~\ref{chap:intro}; Fig.~\ref{fig:zeeman_broadening}).

To model how the field broadens the line profiles, \texttt{ZeeTurbo} solves the polarised radiative transfer equation across the stellar disc using routines adapted from the \texttt{Zeeman} code \citep{landstreet+1988, wade+2001, folsom+2016}. The disc is divided into cells, each assigned a local field strength and orientation, and the line and continuum opacities are computed by \texttt{Turbospectrum} \citep{alvarez_plez+1998, plez_2012}, with the opacity of every $\pi$ and $\sigma$ component evaluated separately before integration over the visible surface. The synthesis uses \textsc{marcs} model atmospheres \citep{gustafsson+2008}. Within each synthesised spectrum the field is taken to have a single strength across the surface and a radial orientation, since the disc-integrated Stokes~$I$ profile is barely changed when the field is switched from radial to azimuthal, meaning that we cannot determine the local field orientation \citep{Kochukhov_2021}. In the pipeline, such single-strength spectra are combined into a distribution of strengths (Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_magnetic}). Rotational and macroturbulent broadening are applied afterwards rather than during the synthesis, keeping them out of the grid so that one grid can be broadened to any rotation and macroturbulence required.

A single \texttt{ZeeTurbo} synthesis is far too slow to run at every step of the Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling. We therefore use a precomputed grid of synthetic spectra spanning the atmospheric parameters and field strengths expected in our sample --- effective temperature $T_\mathrm{eff}$, surface gravity $\log g$, and metallicity $\mathrm{[M/H]}$, with each grid point computed at field strengths from $0$ to $10\,\mathrm{kG}$ in $1\,\mathrm{kG}$ steps --- so that the pipeline only needs to interpolate within it.

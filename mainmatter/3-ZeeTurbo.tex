\section{ZeeTurbo synthetic spectrum grids}
\label{chap:zeeturbo}

\texttt{ZeeTurbo} is a spectral-synthesis code that computes the spectrum of an M~dwarf for a given set of atmospheric parameters and a given surface magnetic field strength \citep{cristofari_2023a}. We use it to generate the synthetic spectra that our retrieval compares against the observations, and in particular to model how a magnetic field reshapes the spectral lines from which we measure that field.

A magnetic field splits each atomic energy level into sublevels, so that a spectral line splits into a central $\pi$ component and a pair of shifted $\sigma$ components. Their separation grows as $g_\mathrm{eff}\, B\, \lambda^2$, where $g_\mathrm{eff}$ is the effective Land\'e factor of the line and $B$ the field strength \citep{donati_landstreet+2009, Reiners+2012, Kochukhov_2021}. We analyse only the unpolarised Stokes~$I$ intensity (Sect.~\ref{sec:obs_instruments}), in which these components are not individually resolved but blend into a single broader, deeper line. This Zeeman intensification is the signal we measure. Because it scales with $g_\mathrm{eff}$, lines of different magnetic sensitivity respond differently to the same field, which is what allows us to separate the field from the other effects that broaden a line.

To capture this, \texttt{ZeeTurbo} solves the polarised radiative transfer equation across the stellar disc, using routines adapted from the \texttt{Zeeman} code \citep{landstreet+1988, wade+2001, folsom+2016}. The disc is divided into cells, each assigned a local field strength and orientation. The continuous and line opacities are computed by \texttt{Turbospectrum} \citep{alvarez_plez+1998, plez_2012}, with the line opacities evaluated separately for every $\pi$ and $\sigma$ component before being integrated over the visible surface. The synthesis is carried out on \textsc{marcs} model atmospheres \citep{gustafsson+2008} and includes a full treatment of molecular lines, which is essential for M~dwarfs, whose spectra are crowded with molecular features that blend with the atomic lines. The field is assumed to be radial and of a single strength over the surface, and rotational and macroturbulent broadening are added afterwards rather than during the synthesis itself.

Rather than run \texttt{ZeeTurbo} during the fit, we precompute a grid of synthetic spectra covering the range of atmospheric parameters and field strengths expected in our sample. The grid spans effective temperature $T_\mathrm{eff}$, surface gravity $\log g$, and metallicity $\mathrm{[M/H]}$, and at each point it is computed for field strengths from $0$ to $10\,\mathrm{kG}$ in steps of $1\,\mathrm{kG}$. The retrieval then only needs to interpolate within this grid, which keeps the analysis fast enough to apply to the full sample.

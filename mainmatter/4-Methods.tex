\section{Methods: characterising spectra with MCMC}
\label{sec:methods}

This chapter describes how we extract atmospheric and magnetic parameters from the master spectra produced in Sect.~\ref{sec:obs_preparation}. The retrieval is a Bayesian MCMC fit against synthetic spectra drawn from the ZeeTurbo grid, with a parameter vector spanning $T_\mathrm{eff}$, $\log g$, $\mathrm{[M/H]}$, $v_\mathrm{mac}$, and a set of discrete filling factors $f_i$ defined on a grid of magnetic field strengths $B_i$ over the $0$--$10\,\mathrm{kG}$ range.

Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_linelist} describes the atomic and molecular line list common to all stars, drawn from the VALD3 database [CITATION NEEDED: vald3]. Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_filter} introduces a per-star, $\chi^2$-based filter that prunes spectral regions in which the forward model fits the data poorly, allowing the use of a uniform initial list across a heterogeneous sample. Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_bic} introduces a post-hoc $\mathrm{BIC}$ scan that selects the number of magnetic filling-factor components per star, in contrast to the fixed magnetic grids used by \citet{cristofari+2022b, cristofari_2023b, reiners_2022}. Sect.~\ref{sec:methods_campaign} closes by describing how these elements are assembled into a three-phase campaign that produces the self-consistent parameter set used in the chapters that follow.

\subsection{The MCMC fit}
\label{sec:methods_fit}
The goal of the MCMC is to search for the model in the grid created by \texttt{ZeeTurbo} that provides the best fit to the observations. For this we rely on the emcee package\footnote{\url{https://emcee.readthedocs.io/en/stable/}} \citep{emcee} to estimate the atmospheric parameters from posterior distributions. At each iteration a set of parameters is defined. With these parameters, synthetic spectra are pulled from the precomputed \texttt{ZeeTurbo} grid and linearly interpolated over the 3D box $(T_\mathrm{eff}, \log g, \mathrm{[M/H]})$ at each magnetic-field node $B_i$, with $i = 0, 1, \dots, N_B$ indexing the discrete field strengths spanning $0$--$10\,\mathrm{kG}$ in $1\,\mathrm{kG}$ steps. This produces $N_B + 1$ per-node spectra $S_i(\lambda; T_\mathrm{eff}, \log g, \mathrm{[M/H]})$ on the wavelength grid of the precomputed grid.
Following the results of \citet{shulyak+2010, shulyak+2014, kochukhov+2020, reiners+2022, cristofari_2023a}, the stellar spectra are modelled as a combination of spectra computed for various magnetic field strengths. These interpolated spectra are then linearly combined in flux weighted by the filling factors $f_i$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:magnetic-mixing}
  S_\mathrm{mag}(\lambda) = \sum_{i=0}^{N_B} f_i \, S_i(\lambda) ,
  \qquad \sum_{i=0}^{N_B} f_i = 1 ,
  \qquad f_i \geq 0 .
\end{equation}
The surface is, thus, treated as a mosaic of magnetic patches with discrete field strengths. Each filling factor is defined to be greater than zero as a negative percentage of the total distribution is both physically and mathematically wrong. The sum of the filling factors are defined to add up to one so the entire surface of the star is spanned.
Next we apply the Doppler shift to the wavelength solution of the synthetic spectrum, which is defined as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}(v_r) \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{1 + \beta}{1 - \beta}} , \qquad \beta \equiv \frac{v_r}{c}.
\end{equation}
Initially, this $v_r$ is found by cross-correlation the observed spectrum $S(\lambda)$ against a VALD absorption line-mask $\{(\lambda_j^{(0)},\, w_j)\}$, where $\lambda_j^{(0)}$ are rest-frame line centres and $w_j$ are the depths of the lines, which are used as line weights. With median-subtracted versions $\tilde w_j = w_j - \operatorname{med}(w)$ and $\tilde S = S - \operatorname{med}(S)$, the normalised CCF at velocity $v$ is
\begin{equation}
    \rho(v) \;=\; \frac{\sum_j \tilde w_j \, \tilde S\!\bigl(\lambda_j^{(0)} \mathcal{D}(-v)\bigr)}{\sqrt{\,\sum_j \tilde w_j^{\,2} \;\; \sum_j \tilde S\!\bigl(\lambda_j^{(0)} \mathcal{D}(-v)\bigr)^{2}\,}} .
\end{equation}
The initial RV values is calculated in two passes; a coarse and a fine pass:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Coarse}: $v \in \{-250,\dots,+250\}$ km s$^{-1}$ at $\Delta v = 2$ km s$^{-1}$. $v_0$ is then the value for which $\rho$ is minimal.
    \item \textbf{Fine}: Pre-shift the mask by $\mathcal{D}(-v_0)$ and then recompute $\rho(v)$ for narrower set of velocities of $\pm 25$ km s$^{-1}$ at $\Delta v = 0.1$ km s$^{-1}$, then cubic-interpolate $\rho$ onto a 0.01 km s$^{-1}$ grid and locate $v_1$.
\end{enumerate}
The returned initial Radial velocity is then equal to $v_\text{RV} =  v_0 + v_1$. This value is then used to shift the wavelength solution of the synthetic spectrum to match the observed radial velocity.

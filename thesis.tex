  \abstract 
  % context heading (optional)
  % {} leave it empty if necessary  
   {
   M dwarfs are the most common stars and planet hosts in the Galaxy, and their small-scale magnetic fields, which carry most of the surface flux and are measurable through Zeeman broadening in unpolarised spectra, bias the stellar parameters derived from those spectra if left unmodelled. We test whether these fields and the atmospheric parameters can be retrieved homogeneously from archival optical spectra, applying a single pipeline identically to every star and benchmarking against published near-infrared values. Using the \texttt{asap} pipeline with \texttt{ZeeTurbo} synthetic spectra, we analyse the Stokes~$I$ spectra of 41 M dwarfs from the NARVAL and ESPaDOnS échelle spectropolarimeters, modelling the field as a distribution of filling factors and selecting the number of magnetic components per star with a Bayesian information criterion that removes a weak-field bias. Our effective temperatures, gravities, and metallicities agree with the literature, and the optical fields reproduce the near-infrared ranking of the most magnetic stars while lying systematically higher, by more than $0.6\,\mathrm{kG}$ on average against SPIRou measurements. A single uniformly applied pipeline thus returns a self-consistent set of atmospheric and magnetic parameters that validates against the near-infrared without per-star tuning, and we suggest that the higher optical magnetic fields may not indicate a modelling defect, but could instead arise from a combination of the differing formation depths, the non-simultaneity of the observations, and the weaker, $\lambda^2$-scaled optical Zeeman signal.
  }
